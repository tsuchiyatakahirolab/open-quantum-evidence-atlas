When governments fund international quantum research, grants and papers are easy to count. The harder question is: what becomes reusable?

Open Quantum Evidence Atlas turns that question into an executable observability audit of the OpenAIRE Graph.

Inside a declared 2020-to-2026 Japan–EU27 boundary, we audited all 645 publications. Project or funding links reach 60.8 percent. Dataset visibility falls to 27.8 percent; software to 7.4. An 8.2-fold cliff.

These are graph measurements, not model estimates. Change the boundary, and the auditor recomputes every denominator and Wilson interval.

A strict title search changes the sample, not the conclusion. Each record is connected, not observable, or not audited. Not observable never means nonexistent.

Seventeen records form complete chains. The featured case traces European Commission funding through three projects and partner institutions to a publication, a Figshare dataset, and two software records. Every step opens its source.

Then we test the interface. Alien's authenticated OpenAIRE MCP confirms the DOI and grant, but returns no rows for nonzero dataset and software totals. The cause: OpenAIRE starts at page zero; the MCP accepts page one or higher. A public reproducer and direct API fallback turn that boundary into a regression test.

A full rerun reproduced all 645 identifiers, every link state, and all four rates. Six uncached checks passed.

Reuse the auditor for any programme, field, or country pair. Keep the broad Atlas for landscapes; use Q-NEKO as a clock for graph-entry lag.
